Prologue
Maison d'horlogerie · Vallée de Joux · 1887

Some hours aremade once.

Follow a single watch from a Swiss mountain to your wrist. Scroll slowly.

IThe valley

In the winter of 1887, a clockmaker stopped counting.

Étienne Aurevant climbed above the snowline with one question: what if a watch were made to be inherited, not replaced? He wintered in a stone workshop. By the thaw, one movement lay on his bench.

IIThe hands

Six hundred and twelve parts. One pair of hands.

Every bridge is bevelled and polished by hand. Every screw is blued over a flame. No two movements are finished on the same day.

IIIThe dial

A face that remembers the sky.

Grand feu enamel, fired five times. A moonphase that drifts by a single day only once in 122 years. The hands rest at ten past ten, so the dial seems to smile.

IVThe night

When the room goes dark, it keeps time.

Every index is lit by hand, one drop at a time. You will not need to look. You will know.

VThe promise

Wear it for a lifetime. Then hand it on.

The atelier that made your watch services it for as long as it runs. We keep the ledger. Your name goes in it, and so does the next.
