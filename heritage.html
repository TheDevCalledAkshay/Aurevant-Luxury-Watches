Est. 1887

A watchmaker who stopped counting.

The story of Aurevant begins with a question: what if a watch were made to be inherited, not replaced?

"I do not make watches for today. I make them for the grandchild of the man who orders one."

— Étienne Aurevant, 1891
1887

The Ascent

Étienne Aurevant, a clockmaker's son from Le Sentier, climbs above the snowline of the Vallée de Joux. He winters in a stone workshop with nothing but his tools, steel, and one question. By the spring thaw, one movement lies on his bench — the first Aurevant calibre.

1902

The First Commission

After fifteen years of refining a single calibre, word reaches Geneva. A collector commissions the first Aurevant wristwatch. Étienne delivers it after fourteen months — a habit the maison keeps to this day.

1934

The Moonphase

Henri Aurevant, Étienne's grandson, introduces the moonphase complication — accurate to within one day in 122 years. The mechanism is so delicate that only one watchmaker in the atelier is entrusted with its assembly.

1968

The Quartz Refusal

As the quartz revolution sweeps through Switzerland, Aurevant is offered licensing deals, partnerships, acquisitions. The family declines them all. The atelier continues with hand-wound movements. Production drops to twelve watches per year.

1987

The Centenary

To mark one hundred years, Aurevant introduces grand feu enamel dials — fired five times in the kiln. Each firing risks cracking the dial. Only the ones that survive are deemed worthy. The three current references — Nox, Aube, Midi — are born.

Today

The Continuation

The atelier still stands above Le Sentier. The ledger still records every watch and every owner. The waiting list is fourteen months. Some things should not be hurried.
